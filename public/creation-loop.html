⚡ How Generation Works

What happens behind the scenes when you press the Generate button.

Expert does not just ask an AI to write once and hand you the result. Instead, it runs a small team of specialised AI roles in a short loop: one writes, one grades the writing against your quality criteria, and one revises the text directly to fix whatever fell short. The loop repeats until the writing is good enough, or until it runs out of attempts and keeps the best version. Each role is a separate "purpose" and can even be a different AI model.

The four roles (purposes)
✍️
Creator
writes structure

Writes the first draft for structural nodes (an outline, a chapter broken into sections).

Model: capable and logical. It does most of the structural thinking, so reasoning matters more than writing flair.

📖
Prose
writes the scene

The writer for the finished, lowest-level text (a leaf scene). Used instead of the Creator when the node is a leaf.

Model: a natural, human tone and strong writing skills. Logic still helps, but the heavy reasoning was already done by the Creator.

🎯
Rater
grades the draft

Scores the draft against each of your quality criteria, giving every one a mark from 1 to 10.

Model: can be smaller and cheaper. Grading against clear criteria is less demanding than writing, so it need not be as smart as the others.

🛠️
Editor
edits the text

Directly revises the draft to fix whatever fell short of its goal, while preserving the parts that already pass. It returns the full corrected text — there is no separate rewrite step.

Model: capabilities close to the Creator/Prose, since it rewrites real text — but it need not be creative. It fixes against the criteria rather than inventing.

Mixing models vs. one model for everything. A single high-end, all-purpose model can fill every role, and that is the simplest setup. But it can be slower and more expensive than you need, and is often overkill — especially for the Rater. Pairing a strong Creator, Prose, and Editor with a lighter Rater often saves time and cost without hurting quality.

Why no specific recommendations? Model strengths shift with every new release, so any concrete list would go stale quickly. Judge each model against the needs above rather than a fixed name.
